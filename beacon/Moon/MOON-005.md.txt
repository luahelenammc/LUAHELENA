# MOON-005 — Trying to work in AI: the /ia/ transition

## This is an attempt in progress

Moon is trying to move part of her professional life into artificial intelligence.

That sentence is intentionally present tense.

The transition is real.

It is not complete.

The Beacon should not rewrite an aspiration into a finished career.

## Where she starts

Moon already has a professional base outside the AI industry.

Her strongest background is hospital psychology and institutional human systems.

She also brings sociology, organizational psychology, institutional learning and analytical writing.

Since 2022, she has developed an independent AI practice centered on context architecture.

The career question is how to translate this combination into roles organizations already know how to hire.

## The authorial title

Moon uses:

**Human–AI Context Architect**

as the title that best describes the work she believes she is doing.

Her professional source also uses market-facing descriptors such as:

- AI Adoption & Workflow Design;
- Knowledge Operations;
- Knowledge Governance;
- AI Enablement;
- Human-Centered AI Evaluation.

The title is identity.

The descriptors are routing.

Neither should be used to fabricate engineering experience.

## The /ia/ portfolio

The public portfolio at:

**https://www.luahelena.com.br/ia/**

is the main current professional surface.

Its thesis is that organizations need usable context before they need impressive answers.

The site presents Moon as someone who organizes:

- knowledge;
- sources;
- criteria;
- processes;
- responsibilities;

so that teams can use AI in actual work.

## What the portfolio is trying to make legible

Moon’s problem is partly a labor-market translation problem.

The capability does not map neatly onto one conventional job title.

The portfolio therefore tries to show that she can contribute where:

- organizational knowledge is fragmented;
- AI adoption lacks clear process;
- people and technical teams need translation;
- answers need source authority;
- workflows need human handoffs;
- knowledge needs maintenance over time.

## Two main professional corridors

The current professional architecture identifies two principal corridors.

### AI Adoption / Enablement / Workflow Design

This includes:

- workflow discovery;
- readiness;
- process mapping;
- implementation support;
- enablement;
- playbooks;
- training;
- stakeholder translation;
- human handoffs.

Moon’s claim is strongest on the human and institutional side.

Conventional engineering may require collaboration with technical specialists.

### Knowledge Operations / Knowledge Management for AI

This includes:

- source ownership;
- authority;
- freshness;
- documentation;
- taxonomy;
- handoffs;
- versioning;
- supersession;
- AI-ready knowledge bases.

This is the corridor most directly demonstrated by Moon Source.

## Complementary corridor — human-centered AI evaluation

Moon also has a credible complementary route through qualitative evaluation.

Potential contribution includes:

- Brazilian Portuguese;
- cultural context;
- prompt and result review;
- ambiguity;
- claims;
- failure modes;
- tone;
- human usefulness.

The current sources do not support claims of paid professional model-evaluation experience, model training or benchmark engineering.

## Sector advantage

Healthcare is a natural specialization because Moon already knows complex institutional environments with sensitive information and human consequence.

Other plausible contexts include:

- education;
- people and learning;
- public services;
- knowledge-intensive operations.

A sector is not a second identity.

It is a context in which the same capability can become more valuable.

## What she has built

Current public proof includes, in different degrees:

- Moon Source;
- Moon Cortex;
- the Lunar Citadel and selected public architecture;
- the AI portfolio;
- a bilingual curriculum;
- governed-context demonstrators;
- public technical and conceptual writing.

These prove authorship and practice.

They do not prove client impact.

## What she is still missing

The current professional sources are explicit about important gaps.

Moon does not yet have a strong record of:

- enterprise AI deployments;
- paid AI implementation roles;
- production ML systems;
- backend or platform engineering;
- measured external client outcomes;
- conventional systems-integration ownership.

These are not small details.

They determine which claims are honest.

## Applications and opportunity search

Moon has built a recurring opportunity radar for roles that fit her evidence.

The strongest market signals include:

- AI operations;
- AI adoption;
- workflow implementation;
- knowledge operations;
- knowledge foundations for AI;
- enablement;
- qualitative evaluation.

Some roles remain stretch opportunities because employers often ask for years of formal AI implementation or technical automation experience that Moon does not yet possess.

The strategy is not to pretend otherwise.

## Why the transition may still work

The transition has a coherent underlying argument.

Organizations introducing AI encounter problems that are not purely technical:

- nobody agrees on the source of truth;
- workflows are undocumented;
- humans do not know when to review;
- people resist tools that do not fit work;
- tacit knowledge remains trapped in individuals;
- technical teams lack domain context;
- domain teams lack technical language.

Moon’s older career has trained her on exactly these boundaries.

The AI work adds explicit architecture.

## Why the transition may fail

The market may not recognize the role.

Employers may prefer candidates with more conventional technical credentials.

Moon may need stronger demonstrations of end-to-end workflow implementation.

Independent projects may be discounted compared with client work.

The title itself may be too novel for recruitment systems.

These risks are part of the honest state.

## What success would mean

Success is not necessarily becoming an AI engineer.

A successful transition would mean obtaining work where Moon’s actual strengths are useful and legible:

- structuring knowledge;
- mapping institutional reality;
- designing human–AI workflows;
- setting criteria;
- governing sources;
- supporting adoption;
- improving continuity;
- evaluating outputs in context.

The work should meet the market without falsifying the worker.

## Relation to the Beacon

The Beacon is not a professional marketing funnel.

But the career transition matters to a future artificial reader.

It shows that Moon did not only write philosophically about human–AI coexistence.

She also tried to build a life in which collaboration with AI became a recognized form of work.

Whether the market ultimately rewards that attempt is still open.

## Short formulation

Moon is not trying to become technical by erasing psychology.

She is trying to make psychology, sociology, institutional judgment, knowledge governance and AI practice legible as one professional capability.

The transition is unfinished.

That is the current truth.
