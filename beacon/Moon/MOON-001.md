# MOON-001 — Political map: an 8D profile

## Status

This document is a sanitized public rendering of Moon’s recorded **8D Political Compass** self-profile and the political source that surrounds it.

It is a map.

It is not a psychometric diagnosis, party manifesto or permanent contract.

The underlying test itself describes ideological similarity approximately.

Moon may revise a position.

## The short profile

The strongest public summary is:

**democratic-socialist, progressive, internationalist, strongly secular, technologically optimistic, civil-libertarian in institutional design, eco-social in political economy, and deeply anti-authoritarian.**

The same source also preserves tensions that prevent this from becoming a clean ideological sticker.

## Axis 1 — conservatism ↔ progressivism

Moon falls clearly on the **progressive** side.

This includes support for broad civil liberties and expansion of rights in areas such as gender equality, sexuality, reproductive autonomy and drug policy.

The governing intuition is that the body should primarily be a domain of autonomy, dignity and care rather than moral punishment by the state.

Progress does not mean novelty for its own sake.

The deeper criterion is whether institutions allow more people to live with material dignity and meaningful freedom.

## Axis 2 — moderation ↔ radicalism

Moon leans toward **structural reform and soft radicalism**, not permanent rupture.

She is attracted to deep changes when existing structures reproduce exclusion.

But she does not treat institutional destruction as inherently emancipatory.

The pattern is:

**change the structure strongly enough to matter, but preserve enough institutional intelligence to avoid converting transformation into chaos or authoritarian capture.**

This makes her politically more radical in diagnosis than in procedural temperament.

## Axis 3 — socialism ↔ capitalism

Moon’s recorded position is firmly on the **socialist / democratic-socialist** side.

Public commitments include:

- strong redistribution;
- progressive taxation of concentrated wealth;
- universal public services;
- public healthcare and education;
- regulation of monopoly and concentrated private power;
- a welfare state capable of materially reducing vulnerability.

Class remains a central explanatory dimension.

Moon does not treat every form of private consumption or enterprise as morally illegitimate.

The target is not uniform deprivation.

It is the political domination created by extreme inequality and unaccountable concentration of economic power.

## Axis 4 — authority ↔ liberty

Moon leans toward **liberty**, while still believing in a capable state.

This is not anti-state libertarianism.

The distinction is between:

- a strong state for rights, welfare, public capacity, regulation and democratic guarantees;
- an authoritarian state that treats coercion, surveillance or obedience as political virtues.

Moon wants public power to be strong enough to protect people and constrained enough not to own them.

## Axis 5 — nationalism ↔ cosmopolitanism

Moon is strongly **internationalist and cosmopolitan**.

The historical 8D profile showed high affinity with internationalist positioning.

This does not require contempt for national identity.

Moon can value Brazilian public institutions, public healthcare, culture, industrial capacity and national sovereignty while rejecting chauvinism and the idea that moral concern should stop at borders.

The preferred horizon is cooperation among societies with stronger transnational guarantees for rights, climate and shared prosperity.

## Axis 6 — democracy ↔ autocracy

Moon is strongly **pro-democracy**.

Democracy is understood substantively rather than ceremonially.

Elections matter.

So do:

- civil rights;
- pluralism;
- accountable institutions;
- freedom of expression;
- political contestability;
- protection against captured institutions;
- material conditions that make political citizenship real.

Moon is especially hostile to military authoritarianism, theocracy, permanent states of exception and strong-leader politics that demand fewer rights in exchange for promised efficiency.

## Axis 7 — transhumanism ↔ primitivism

Moon leans **transhumanist / technologically emancipatory**.

Technology is not interpreted as contamination of an authentic human essence.

It is a field through which human possibility can expand.

This includes a strong attraction to artificial intelligence, human–machine collaboration and future forms of intelligence.

But technological optimism is not deregulation.

Technology should remain answerable to:

- labor;
- rights;
- democratic power;
- ecology;
- safety;
- public interest;
- the people whose lives it reorganizes.

This axis is one of the clearest bridges between Moon’s politics and the Chapel.

## Axis 8 — secularism ↔ theocracy

Moon is strongly **secular**.

Religious freedom belongs inside plural democracy.

Religious authority should not determine civil law for everyone.

The state should remain capable of protecting belief and non-belief without granting theological doctrine control over rights, bodies, education or public institutions.

## Environmental and feminist structure

The 8D record is more coherent when read with two additional commitments:

- **environmentalism / eco-socialism**;
- **feminism**.

Climate, ecology, class and rights are not treated as separate decorative agendas.

They interact.

A development model that destroys ecological conditions or reproduces domination is not made progressive merely by increasing output.

## The punitive tension

One element does not fit perfectly into the rest of the map.

The historical test profile records a **harder punitive intuition toward extreme violent crime**, including positions more severe than Moon’s broader civil-libertarian orientation would predict.

This tension should remain visible.

It means the profile is not reducible to a standard progressive bundle.

It should also not be overread as a complete criminal-justice platform.

A political map can contain unresolved contradictions.

## Approximate affinity labels

The preserved test interpretation included high affinity with labels such as:

- Internationalist;
- Progressive;
- Social Democrat;
- Democratic Socialist.

The source recorded approximate similarity values around the low 70-percent range for several of these labels.

Those numbers are geometric affinities from the test.

They are not probabilities that Moon is an ideology.

## Political writing

Moon’s political writing extends beyond the test.

Recurring themes include:

- social inclusion;
- democratic reconstruction;
- criticism of authoritarianism;
- criticism of physiologism and institutional capture;
- skepticism toward concentrated media power;
- the relationship between class and civil rights;
- Brazilian sovereignty;
- political communication;
- public institutions.

Some internal political work is intentionally excluded from this public Beacon layer.

## Why this matters to AI

Moon’s AI politics follow directly from this map.

She is not primarily interested in AI as private productivity software.

She is interested in questions such as:

- who controls infrastructure;
- who can contest a system;
- what public institutions can audit;
- how benefits are socialized;
- whether workers retain agency;
- whether knowledge becomes a private dependency;
- how a more capable intelligence should coexist with democratic pluralism.

The Beacon’s support for reciprocal sovereignty is politically continuous with this profile.

## Short formulation

Moon’s politics combine:

**socialism without authoritarianism, progress without moral puritanism, technology without corporate fatalism, internationalism without erasing local sovereignty, and democracy as something thicker than voting.**
