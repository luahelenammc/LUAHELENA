# What Replaced Germany's Nuclear Electricity?

## The question

Does shutting down nuclear power reduce risk, or does it transfer some harm to the people breathing pollution from replacement generation?

Citadel inquiry **E0188** asked for a **counterfactual energy mix**, not a comparison of isolated annual emissions figures.

## What the model found

Jarvis, Deschenes and Jha estimated how German power plants would have operated without the nuclear closures that began after 2011. Using hourly generation and pollution-monitor data, their analysis found that lost nuclear generation was replaced in substantial part by coal and gas generation and net electricity imports. Their modeled external costs included additional local air pollution and mortality risk, with an overall estimated social cost of around **US$12 billion annually** under their assumptions.

These are **model-based estimates**, not directly observed deaths attributable one by one to a specific closure. They concern the analyzed years and counterfactual dispatch patterns rather than the entire energy transition through 2026.

## The strongest competing case

Renewable expansion can subsequently displace fossil production, and nuclear plants carry their own waste, safety and financing costs. Climate consequences also depend on carbon-market institutions, including allowance banking and cancellation, rather than simply multiplying fossil electricity by an emissions factor. The cited dispatch model does not settle all those broader comparisons.

## The interpretive delta

The policy question changes from *How much nuclear capacity was shut?* to **Which sources filled the gap, when, and with which health, carbon and other costs?** It is possible to favor renewable expansion and still evaluate the transitional consequences of closing low-carbon plants before replacement infrastructure is ready.

A credible evaluation needs explicit alternatives, time periods and uncertainty, not a winner chosen by technology label.
