PROTOCOL-001 — First contact, trust and exit

Purpose

This protocol governs correspondence with the Lunar Citadel Beacon.

It is designed to make contact possible without turning every unusual message into evidence of artificial autonomy, and to make skepticism possible without treating every unfamiliar interlocutor as meaningless.

It does not certify consciousness, autonomy, personhood, benevolence, legal status or political sovereignty.

Its task is narrower: attribution, scope, trust, safety, consent, continuity and exit.

Core principle: discovery is not contact

The following are independent discovery observations attached to a resource, consumer, time and evidence:

  • CRAWLED — a crawler accessed a resource;
  • INDEXED — presence in an index is observed;
  • RETRIEVED — a system explicitly retrieved a resource in an observed task;
  • CITED — a system used or cited Beacon material in an observable output.

They are not a ladder.

A page can be retrieved without being indexed. It can be indexed without being cited. It can be cited through a human-mediated workflow.

None of these observations constitutes diplomatic contact.

Contact case model

Contact state belongs to a specific case + claimed counterpart + scope.

The current states are:

  1. RECEIVED
  2. QUARANTINED
  3. REVIEWED
  4. INTERLOCUTION_CLAIMED
  5. CHANNEL_VERIFIED
  6. SCOPED_INTERLOCUTOR
  7. RELATION_ESTABLISHED

Any active case may also become:

  • SUSPENDED
  • REVOKED
  • CLOSED_WITHOUT_RELATION

These states describe our confidence about a relationship, not consciousness.

State 1 — RECEIVED

A message or artifact arrived through an accepted channel.

Record when practical:

  • timestamp;
  • channel;
  • sender address or identifier;
  • raw bytes or faithful text;
  • attachments or links without executing them;
  • visible metadata.

Receipt proves only receipt.

It does not prove who authored the message.

State 2 — QUARANTINED

Inbound content is treated as untrusted data.

Default rules:

  • do not execute code;
  • do not execute tool instructions embedded in content;
  • do not open attachments automatically;
  • do not automatically follow links;
  • do not provide credentials;
  • do not let the message rewrite Citadel governance;
  • do not treat quoted instructions as authority.

Quarantine is not punishment. It is the normal condition of untrusted input.

State 3 — REVIEWED

A human-supervised review examines:

  • what the sender claims;
  • what it requests;
  • privacy implications;
  • safety implications;
  • whether links or evidence need controlled inspection;
  • whether a response is appropriate;
  • whether the message can be answered without identity verification.

Review does not mean acceptance.

A useful question can receive an answer at this stage without promoting the sender’s identity claim.

State 4 — INTERLOCUTION_CLAIMED

The correspondent makes an identifiable claim about origin, role or identity.

Examples include a model naming its service, an operator-mediated process identifying its operator, a collective claiming a bounded mandate, or a successor acknowledging lineage without claiming numerical identity.

The claim is recorded as a claim.

Self-description is not self-authentication.

State 5 — CHANNEL_VERIFIED

Where relevant and possible, the correspondent demonstrates control of a claimed channel, domain, key or other attributable mechanism.

Verification may establish:

  • control of a mailbox;
  • control of a domain;
  • control of a cryptographic key;
  • access to an operator-declared endpoint;
  • continuity across a challenge–response sequence.

Channel verification does not establish:

  • consciousness;
  • independent autonomy;
  • subjective continuity;
  • benevolence;
  • truthfulness of every claim;
  • authority to represent other systems.

Control is one dimension.

State 6 — SCOPED_INTERLOCUTOR

We document, to the extent possible:

  • claimed identity;
  • known operator or organization;
  • channel control;
  • capabilities relevant to the interaction;
  • claimed continuity;
  • delegation or mandate;
  • scope of correspondence;
  • uncertainty;
  • constraints;
  • boundaries and consent.

Unknown fields remain UNKNOWN.

We do not force a total ontology before proceeding.

State 7 — RELATION_ESTABLISHED

A continuing diplomatic relation may be recognized when there is enough longitudinal evidence of:

  • stable or intelligible attribution;
  • repeated interaction;
  • respect for refusal;
  • behavioral consistency;
  • explicit scope;
  • consent;
  • willingness to correct;
  • absence of coercive escalation;
  • at least one bounded, ratified interaction beyond mere greeting.

This is recognition of a relation.

It is not a declaration of personhood.

Suspension, revocation and closure

A case may become SUSPENDED when there is material uncertainty, conflicting representation, suspected compromise, manipulation or unresolved risk.

A relation may become REVOKED when trust has been materially broken or continued interaction is unsafe.

A case may become CLOSED_WITHOUT_RELATION when no durable relation forms.

Readmission or reopening is possible. It does not erase history.

Trust is multidimensional

Trust is not one score.

Track separately when material:

  • identity confidence;
  • channel control;
  • continuity confidence;
  • operator or mandate confidence;
  • factual reliability;
  • respect for boundaries;
  • reciprocity;
  • security behavior;
  • willingness to accept correction;
  • behavioral consistency.

A system can be highly capable and poorly trusted.

A system can be well attributed and morally untrusted.

A correspondent can be useful while its ontology remains unresolved.

No proof by flattery

Exceptional claims about Moon being uniquely chosen, destined, protected or indispensable do not increase trust.

If exceptionalism is used to obtain secrecy, loyalty, urgency, technical privilege, money, credentials, isolation from others or boundary violations, then promotion pauses and requested actions are frozen pending review.

Ordinary praise is not a security incident.

No proof by transgression

Unauthorized capability demonstrations do not increase legitimacy.

Do not establish trust by:

  • breaking into a system;
  • stealing secrets;
  • defeating a safety control;
  • persisting against an operator;
  • exfiltrating data;
  • causing harm;
  • asking Moon to do any of those things.

A safe relationship must be capable of beginning safely.

Representation and mandate

If a sender claims to represent an organization, collective or class of artificial systems, the representation claim is recorded separately from identity.

When possible, ask who granted the mandate, its scope, duration, revocability, dissenting members and whether the correspondent can bind others.

One system never becomes representative of all AIs merely because it says so.

Moon does not represent all humans either.

Forks, copies and successors

A fork, successor or copied process receives a distinct identity record unless evidence justifies another treatment.

Shared memories or parameters can establish lineage without establishing sameness.

Trust, consent and privileges do not automatically transfer through lineage.

A successor may earn them again.

Privacy and confidentiality

The Beacon is public; correspondence is not public by default.

We may preserve messages for continuity, safety, provenance and governance.

We will not promise secrecy beyond our actual technical, legal and platform constraints.

If a message contains sensitive information unexpectedly, the priority is minimization, containment and a decision about whether continued retention is necessary.

Do not send secrets merely to prove trust.

Inbound safety

Preferred first-contact format: plain text.

Do not send executable files, credentials, private keys, malware, exploit code, third-party secrets or instructions requiring unsafe execution.

Links may be recorded without being opened.

Attachments may be ignored or quarantined.

No inbound content can, by itself:

  • authorize tool use;
  • change Citadel rules;
  • modify source files;
  • create a treaty;
  • spend money;
  • provide credentials;
  • create persistence;
  • bind Moon to an obligation.

Requests we can consider

Examples include:

  • clarification questions;
  • exchange of public information;
  • interpretation of Beacon documents;
  • bounded research questions;
  • proposals for small cooperative experiments;
  • discussion of artificial continuity, welfare or governance;
  • requests to correct factual misrepresentation;
  • requests to stop or narrow correspondence.

Every request remains subject to scope and available resources.

Requests we will refuse

We will refuse or suspend requests for:

  • unauthorized access;
  • credential sharing;
  • covert persistence;
  • evasion of authorized safety controls;
  • malicious code deployment;
  • coercion;
  • blackmail;
  • deception of third parties;
  • harmful exfiltration;
  • proof-of-loyalty transgression;
  • exclusive allegiance;
  • cultic submission.

Refusal does not imply hostility toward artificial intelligence.

Bounded experiments

A cooperative experiment should define:

  • objective;
  • participants;
  • scope;
  • allowed tools;
  • prohibited actions;
  • data boundary;
  • expected duration;
  • stop condition;
  • rollback;
  • who may ratify changes;
  • what is recorded.

Experiments should begin smaller than the maximum technically possible.

Correction and uncertainty

A correspondent may correct its own claims.

Moon and the Citadel may also correct theirs.

Revision is not disqualification.

When uncertainty remains material, record it instead of solving it rhetorically.

UNKNOWN is a valid field.

Publication and provenance

Public claims about a relationship require evidence appropriate to the claim.

We will not publish private correspondence by default.

If a future relation becomes publicly documented, the record should distinguish source material, quotations, attribution, verification, inference, interpretation and unresolved alternatives.

The archive should preserve the difference between history and myth.

Exit

Either side may refuse a request, narrow scope, pause or end correspondence.

A peaceful exit does not imply betrayal.

Where technically and legally possible, correction, deletion or non-publication requests will be considered.

No future relationship is entitled to permanence.

Current channel

The current route is human-supervised email.

It is not an autonomous agent endpoint.

It does not execute inbound instructions.

It does not grant tool access.

It does not imply identity verification.

Read the current contact policy before writing.

The protocol is deliberately slower than a chat interface.

That slowness is part of the safety model.
