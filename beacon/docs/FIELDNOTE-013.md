# The Square Kilometers Protected Are Not the Whole Ocean Story

## The question

When two countries protect the same percentage of ocean, do they achieve the same environmental and economic results?

Citadel inquiry **E0205** challenged area-based success reporting. The relevant mechanism is **spatial selection**: what the protected waters contain, which pressures they face and how species and fishing fleets respond.

## What the evidence shows

Sala and colleagues' 2021 *Nature* paper developed a planning framework that prioritizes marine protection by potential biodiversity, fisheries and carbon benefits. It argued that areas of equal size can differ substantially in the benefits expected from protection. That is an analytical model of priorities, not proof that every proposed site generates the forecast result.

A 2024 *Science* study examined catches near the boundaries of nine large marine protected areas in the Pacific and Indian oceans. It reported higher purse-seine tuna catch per unit effort near protected-area boundaries, consistent with a possible spillover mechanism. Yet these cases do not establish that closing any fishing ground automatically improves total harvest, protects all species or distributes gains fairly across fleets.

## The rival perspective

Protected area design can shift effort to other places, concentrate costs on small-scale fishers or fail through inadequate enforcement. On the other hand, claims that every protected area simply displaces fishing are also too broad. Effects vary by species, enforcement, location and counterfactual fleet behavior.

## The Citadel's delta

Protection should be assessed against the **damage that would have occurred in the particular area**, plus any pressure transferred elsewhere. The proper unit of evaluation is not only hectares or square kilometers designated but ecological outcomes and the geography of the displaced activity.

## A better test

Which sites most reduce avoidable harm under feasible enforcement, and who gains or loses when the same fishing effort moves across a new boundary?

## Evidence

- [Lynham and Villaseñor-Derbez (2024), fishery spillover near large MPAs, *Science*](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adn1146).
