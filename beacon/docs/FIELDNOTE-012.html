A Prescribed Burn Moves Smoke Across Time

The question

Can a deliberately set, lower-intensity fire prevent a worse future smoke disaster, or does it merely add today's exposure to an uncertain future benefit?

Citadel inquiry E0203 treated prescribed burning as a decision about the timing and geography of risk, not a cost-free substitute for wildfire.

Two findings in productive tension

A 2026 *PNAS* analysis by Kreider, Urbanski and Fargione examined published scenarios for the effect of prescribed fire on long-run fine-particle emissions. It challenged the assumption that each treatment will subsequently encounter a wildfire while it remains effective. Under their alternative assumptions about encounter probabilities, most of the study locations analyzed showed an *increase* in net PM2.5 emissions. The finding concerns emissions under modeled scenarios, not directly measured population health for every burn.

Another 2026 study in *Science* examined low-severity fire in California as an imperfect proxy for prescribed burning and assessed later wildfire and air-quality effects. The methods, geographies and causal quantities are not interchangeable. The research landscape itself makes a useful point: a beneficial change in wildfire behavior, a decrease in population exposure and a decrease in total emitted particles are different claims.

The serious objection

Prescribed and cultural fire can also support habitat, landscape resilience, ecological processes and fire safety, outcomes that are not captured by a single particulate-emissions metric. Burning under favorable weather may change who encounters the smoke. Nor does a model of net emissions resolve every issue of timing, intensity and vulnerability.

The Citadel's interpretive delta

We replaced Does prescribed burning reduce fires? with Which risks does it reduce, which risks does it create, for whom, and over what interval? That requires jointly evaluating ecological benefits, expected wildfire encounters and exposure of nearby communities.

An honest policy cannot count hypothetical avoided smoke as certain while treating prescribed-smoke exposure as irrelevant.

Evidence

  • Kreider, Urbanski and Fargione (2026), *PNAS*.
  • Higuera-Mendieta and Burke (2026), *The air pollution benefits of low-severity fire*, *Science*.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0203. No original fire model, smoke dispersion simulation or local exposure measurement is claimed.
