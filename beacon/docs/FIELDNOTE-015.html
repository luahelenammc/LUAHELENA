A Heat Pump Can Cut Carbon While Its Refrigerant Still Matters

The question

Can a technology produce a large overall climate benefit while carrying a serious environmental problem inside its own components?

Citadel inquiry E0220 considered heat pumps: efficient electric heating systems that reduce dependence on combustion but can leak high-global-warming-potential refrigerants.

What the evidence distinguishes

The International Energy Agency's 2022 *Future of Heat Pumps* report found that replacing gas boilers with heat pumps generally lowers greenhouse-gas emissions. Its executive summary reported a minimum 20% reduction for heat pumps using then-current refrigerants, even under emissions-intensive electricity in the settings analyzed; larger reductions were possible in cleaner power systems. Those estimates are scenarios and technology comparisons, not an invariant for every installation anywhere.

The IEA also identified leakage of fluorinated refrigerants as a reason that otherwise substantial climate benefits can be reduced. Refrigerant type, quantity, installation quality, servicing, recovery and final disposal change the lifecycle balance. Low-global-warming-potential alternatives can improve outcomes, subject to performance and safety conditions.

A counterargument worth preserving

Focusing disproportionately on refrigerant leakage may delay beneficial replacement of inefficient combustion heating. Conversely, a decarbonization policy that ignores refrigerant recovery misses an avoidable source of greenhouse gases. The comparative share of leakage emissions may rise as electricity becomes cleaner without the absolute leak emissions themselves increasing.

The Citadel's delta

We rejected the choice between electrify and control refrigerants. They are complementary responsibilities. An equipment transition should track both the emissions avoided by efficient heating and the new lifecycle obligations created by the refrigerant circuit.

A better test

For each system over its expected lifetime, how much combustion-related emissions is avoided, how much refrigerant is lost, and what governance ensures responsible recovery at end of life?

Evidence

  • IEA (2022), *The Future of Heat Pumps*.
  • IEA executive summary, comparative emissions and refrigerant leakage.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0220. No original equipment testing or carbon lifecycle model was produced by the Citadel.
