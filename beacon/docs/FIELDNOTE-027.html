A Participatory Budget Can Survive in Name and Disappear in Power

The question

If the meetings and regulations of participatory budgeting remain, has participatory power actually survived a change of government?

Citadel inquiry E0199 distinguished institutional continuity of form from continued influence over resources. It is related to, but distinct from, E0213, which examined elite control *within* participatory organizations.

What the Porto Alegre record reveals

A 2026 historical-institutional study of Porto Alegre described the gradual erosion of the city's participatory-budgeting model across changes in coalitions, administrative organization, fiscal constraints and civil-society mobilization. The institution did not simply vanish in a single event. Its capacity to organize consequential citizen influence weakened while some formal features remained.

An earlier study of Porto Alegre after the Workers' Party era similarly described administrations that maintained rhetorical support while reducing meaningful administrative and financial backing. Neither historical analysis establishes a universal causal effect of alternation alone: budget constraints and political strategy may interact.

The counterargument

Participatory arrangements can endure across governing coalitions when public legitimacy, credible administrative procedures and organized constituencies support them. Formal legal recognition can help, but a statute alone cannot guarantee resources, implementation or a responsive executive.

The interpretive delta

Institutional survival is not measured by counting meetings or showing that a participation office still has a name. It requires tracking which budget decisions citizens can affect and whether those decisions are executed.

The better question is: after political alternation or fiscal stress, what remains binding, funded and contestable by participants?

Sources

  • Gugliano (2026), *From Innovation to Erosion: The Dismantling of Participatory Budgeting in Porto Alegre*.
  • *A Time of Closure? Participatory Budgeting in Porto Alegre after the Workers' Party Era*, Cambridge.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0199. Interpretive synthesis of public institutional studies, not an evaluation conducted by the Citadel in Porto Alegre.
