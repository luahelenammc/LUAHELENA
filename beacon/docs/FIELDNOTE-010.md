# An Efficient Algorithm Can Still Be an Unaccountable Manager

## The question

Can software improve workplace coordination while making decisions harder for workers to understand or contest? Citadel inquiry **E0194** rejected the assumption that productivity and legitimate authority automatically rise together.

## The evidence in its proper scope

An OECD survey reported in December 2025 gathered data from more than 6,000 mid-level managers in six countries. Many respondents perceived improved speed, information and decision quality from algorithmic management systems. Yet nearly two-thirds of managers using these tools expressed concerns about their effects on workers. Unclear accountability for bad decisions and difficulty following the logic of decisions were prominent concerns.

These are **reported managerial perceptions**, not a randomized demonstration of productivity gains or a measurement of how every affected worker experiences the technology. The survey concerns a defined set of countries and technologies, not all digitally managed workplaces.

## The strongest objection

In opaque or inconsistent organizations, standardized software can sometimes make decisions more predictable and provide better documentation. Human managers are not automatically fairer than algorithms. With genuine participation, accessible records and meaningful appeals, digital tools may strengthen rather than weaken fairness.

## The interpretive delta

The governing metric should not stop at faster assignments or more efficient scheduling. A legitimate system must be answerable to those it governs. The relevant questions are **Who can inspect a decision? Who can challenge it? Who has power to correct the underlying rule?**

The Citadel's contribution is an institutional distinction: the performance of a managerial system and the contestability of its authority are separate dimensions. An efficiency win cannot, by itself, certify procedural justice.

## An open test

When an algorithm changes someone's pay, hours or workload, can that person obtain an explanation and effective review before the harm becomes irreversible?
