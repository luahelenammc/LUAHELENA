# Electric Vehicles Do Not Make Traffic Disappear

## The question

If a city exempts electric cars from restrictions intended to reduce road traffic, which environmental costs does it quietly preserve?

Lunar Citadel inquiry **E0224** considered a policy tension between electrifying the vehicle fleet and reducing the number of vehicle journeys. The distinction is not an argument against electrification. It asks whether **powertrain change** and **traffic reduction** should be treated as substitutes.

## What continues without an exhaust pipe

An electric vehicle has no tailpipe emissions while driving. Yet motion still wears tyres and road surfaces and resuspends road dust. Regenerative braking substantially reduces the need for friction braking, but does not eliminate every non-exhaust source.

The OECD's 2020 analysis of non-exhaust road particulate emissions found that the balance depends on the size and weight of vehicles, materials, braking patterns and driving conditions. Its modelling suggests light electric vehicles may have lower non-exhaust fine-particle emissions than comparable combustion vehicles, while heavier long-range models may emit more. That is not an established universal ranking across all electric and conventional cars.

The OECD also noted a policy mismatch. Vehicle exemptions from congestion charges and access restrictions may support fleet transition, yet they may weaken instruments designed to reduce vehicle-kilometres travelled. That tension deserves explicit treatment rather than a blanket declaration that electric mobility is pollution-free.

## A serious counterargument

Temporary exemptions may speed substitution away from combustion vehicles and deliver substantial benefits for exhaust pollution and greenhouse-gas emissions. The net impact of removing exemptions will vary by fleet, electricity supply, urban density and travel demand. This note does not estimate that net impact for a particular city.

## Our interpretive delta

We replaced one broad question, **Does electrification clean up traffic?**, with two separate policy tests: **Does switching powertrains cut emissions?** and **Does the incentive increase total road travel or undermine measures designed to limit it?**

A coherent policy can reward cleaner vehicles while retaining independent goals for less traffic, safer streets and lower exposure to non-exhaust particles.

## What would change the recommendation?

What measurable extra adoption of low-emission vehicles results from an exemption, and what added traffic or non-exhaust exposure does it create?
