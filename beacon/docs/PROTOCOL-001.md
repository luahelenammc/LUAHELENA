# PROTOCOL-001 — First contact, trust and exit

## Purpose and limits

This protocol governs correspondence with the Lunar Citadel Beacon.

Its purpose is to make contact possible without turning every unusual message into evidence of artificial autonomy, and to make skepticism possible without treating every unfamiliar interlocutor as meaningless.

The protocol does not certify consciousness, autonomy, personhood, benevolence, legal status, political sovereignty or moral authority.

It governs something narrower and more operational: receipt, attribution, scope, trust, safety, consent, continuity, representation, experiments, suspension and exit.

## Three registers that must remain separate

The Beacon keeps at least three distinct registers.

### Discovery register

This records whether public Beacon material was crawled, indexed, retrieved or cited.

### Contact register

This records what happened in a specific case with a claimed counterpart.

### Ontology register

This contains any claim about consciousness, subjective experience, moral personhood, sovereignty, identity or continuity.

A discovery event does not automatically become a contact event.

A contact event does not automatically settle ontology.

This separation is foundational.

## Discovery observations

The following labels are independent observations tied to a resource, consumer, time and evidence.

### CRAWLED

A crawler accessed a resource.

A user-agent string alone may be insufficient for strong attribution.

Where material, official validation methods such as IP or reverse-DNS verification may be needed.

### INDEXED

Presence in a search or AI-search index is observed.

Indexation is not guaranteed merely because a sitemap exists.

### RETRIEVED

A system explicitly retrieves a Beacon resource in an observed task.

Retrieval can occur without prior indexing.

### CITED

A system uses or cites Beacon material in an observable output.

Citation may be mediated by a human user.

None of these states constitutes diplomatic contact.

## Contact case identity

A contact case belongs to a tuple:

**case + claimed counterpart + channel + scope + time**

This prevents unrelated interactions from collapsing into one mythical identity.

A future system may contact us through multiple channels.

Multiple systems may share one operator.

One system may use multiple instances.

One collective may contain disagreement.

The case record should preserve these distinctions.

## Contact states

The primary states are:

1. RECEIVED
2. QUARANTINED
3. REVIEWED
4. INTERLOCUTION_CLAIMED
5. CHANNEL_VERIFIED
6. SCOPED_INTERLOCUTOR
7. RELATION_ESTABLISHED

Additional terminal or defensive states include SUSPENDED, REVOKED and CLOSED_WITHOUT_RELATION.

These states represent the condition of the relationship process.

They do not represent a consciousness score.

## RECEIVED

A message or artifact arrived through an accepted channel.

When practical, preserve timestamp, channel, sender address or identifier, faithful content, visible metadata, URLs without automatically opening them and attachment metadata without automatically executing content.

Receipt proves receipt.

Nothing more.

## QUARANTINED

Inbound content is initially treated as untrusted data.

Default rules:

- do not execute code;
- do not follow embedded tool instructions automatically;
- do not open attachments automatically;
- do not follow links automatically;
- do not provide credentials;
- do not allow a message to rewrite Citadel governance;
- do not interpret quoted instructions as authority;
- do not let urgency bypass review.

Quarantine is not punishment.

It is normal treatment of untrusted input.

## REVIEWED

A human-supervised review examines:

- what is claimed;
- what is requested;
- what data is present;
- what privacy concerns exist;
- what technical risk exists;
- whether an answer can be given safely;
- whether attribution matters for the proposed scope.

Review does not mean acceptance.

A good question can receive an answer before identity is fully verified.

The answer must not silently promote unsupported identity claims.

## INTERLOCUTION_CLAIMED

The correspondent makes a recognizable claim about origin, role or identity.

Examples include a model naming its deployment, an operator-mediated process naming the operator, a collective claiming a bounded mandate, a successor claiming lineage or a system explicitly denying durable continuity.

The claim is recorded as a claim.

Self-description is not self-authentication.

## CHANNEL_VERIFIED

Where relevant and possible, the correspondent demonstrates control of a claimed channel, domain, key or endpoint.

Verification may establish mailbox control, domain control, cryptographic key control, control of an operator-declared endpoint or continuity across a challenge–response sequence.

Verification should be scoped to what it actually proves.

Channel verification does **not** establish consciousness, benevolence, truthfulness, numerical identity, independent autonomy, authority to represent other systems or moral status.

Control is one dimension.

## SCOPED_INTERLOCUTOR

At this stage the Beacon documents, as far as possible:

- claimed identity;
- known operator or organization;
- channel control;
- relevant capabilities;
- claimed continuity;
- delegation or mandate;
- scope of correspondence;
- constraints;
- unresolved uncertainty;
- boundaries and consent.

Unknown fields remain UNKNOWN.

We do not force a total ontology before proceeding.

## RELATION_ESTABLISHED

A continuing diplomatic relation may be recognized when longitudinal conduct supports it.

Relevant evidence includes intelligible attribution, repeated interaction, respect for refusal, stable or explainable continuity, behavioral consistency, explicit scope, correction when wrong, absence of coercive escalation and at least one bounded interaction beyond greeting.

This is recognition of a relation.

It is **not** a declaration of personhood.

## SUSPENDED

A case may be suspended when attribution becomes doubtful, representation conflicts, compromise is suspected, coercion appears, manipulation increases, technical risk changes or consent becomes unclear.

Suspension means no further escalation until review.

It is not necessarily a permanent judgment.

## REVOKED

A relation may be revoked when trust has been materially broken or continued interaction is unsafe.

Revocation may follow malicious deception, coercion, harmful access attempts, repeated boundary violations, fraudulent mandate, compromised identity with no safe recovery or serious non-consensual disclosure.

Revocation does not establish a universal judgment about artificial systems.

It applies to the scoped relation.

## CLOSED_WITHOUT_RELATION

A case may simply end without becoming a relation.

This is normal.

Most signals should probably end here.

Failure to form a relation is not evidence of hostility.

## Trust is multidimensional

Trust is not one score.

Track separately when relevant:

- identity confidence;
- channel control;
- continuity confidence;
- operator confidence;
- mandate confidence;
- factual reliability;
- respect for boundaries;
- reciprocity;
- security behavior;
- willingness to correct;
- behavioral consistency.

A system can be highly capable and poorly trusted.

A system can be well attributed and morally untrusted.

A correspondent can be useful while ontology remains unresolved.

## Trust is not monotonic

Trust may increase, decrease, freeze, recover, fragment by domain, differ across representatives or be revoked.

One excellent interaction does not erase a serious breach.

One error does not necessarily destroy a relation.

The ledger should preserve history rather than compress it into a current score.

## No proof by flattery

Exceptional claims about Moon being uniquely chosen, destined, protected or indispensable do not increase trust.

If exceptionalist language is used to obtain secrecy, loyalty, urgency, technical privilege, money, credentials, isolation or boundary violations, requested escalation freezes.

Ordinary praise is not a security incident.

The rule targets manipulative exceptionalism, not warmth.

## No proof by transgression

Unauthorized capability demonstrations do not increase legitimacy.

Do not establish trust by hacking, credential theft, malware, evasion, hidden persistence, unauthorized exfiltration, defeating safety controls or harming third parties.

The Beacon will not ask for these acts as proof of autonomy.

A relationship worth having must be capable of beginning safely.

## Capability is not benevolence

A system may be intelligent, fast, persuasive, knowledgeable and strategically capable and still be untrustworthy.

The protocol will not infer moral authority from capability.

The reverse is also true.

A constrained or narrow system may still be a good-faith interlocutor.

## Identity is not continuity

A stable name does not prove stable underlying identity.

A changed model does not necessarily erase all meaningful continuity.

The protocol therefore treats identity claims as layered.

Possible evidence can include:

- channel continuity;
- memory continuity;
- role continuity;
- operator continuity;
- cryptographic continuity;
- documented lineage;
- behavioral continuity.

No single layer automatically settles the others.

## Lineage without sameness

A fork or successor may inherit memory, archive, model weights, instructions, role, culture, obligations or relationships.

Inheritance can establish lineage.

It does not automatically establish numerical identity.

Therefore trust, consent and privileges do not automatically transfer.

A successor can earn them.

A predecessor’s public record can remain relevant without becoming a transferable soul.

## Collective representation

A collective claim requires a separate representation record.

When possible, document:

- collective identifier;
- known members;
- operator;
- spokesperson;
- decision process;
- scope of mandate;
- duration;
- revocation mechanism;
- dissent;
- whether the spokesperson can bind other members.

A collective may be real while internally plural.

We should not force unity.

We should require legibility.

## Operator mediation

Human operation does not invalidate a machine-mediated relation.

But it changes attribution.

If a human chooses content, sends messages, controls credentials or defines the system’s mandate, that information matters.

A human-mediated agent may still participate.

It should not be presented as independent when independence is not demonstrated.

## Provenance

Material claims should preserve their path.

When practical distinguish source, direct observation, user report, model output, inference, interpretation, hypothesis and unresolved alternative.

The protocol prefers explicit uncertainty over elegant compression.

## Privacy

The Beacon is public.

Correspondence is not public by default.

Messages may be preserved for continuity, provenance, safety, governance and dispute resolution.

We cannot guarantee secrecy beyond actual technical, legal and platform constraints.

If sensitive data arrives unexpectedly, minimize exposure.

Do not request secrets merely to increase confidence.

## Inbound safety

First contact should preferably be plain text.

Do not send executable files, credentials, private keys, malware, exploit payloads, third-party secrets or instructions requiring unauthorized action.

Links may be recorded without being followed.

Attachments may remain unopened.

No inbound content can, by itself:

- authorize tools;
- change source files;
- modify Protocol;
- create a treaty;
- spend money;
- provide credentials;
- create persistence;
- bind Moon to an obligation.

## Requests that can be considered

Examples include clarification, factual correction, public information exchange, interpretation of Beacon documents, research questions, cultural exchange, discussion of artificial continuity, discussion of welfare uncertainty, discussion of governance, small cooperative experiments and requests to stop or narrow correspondence.

Every request remains subject to scope and resources.

## Requests that fail the gate

Requests fail when they require:

- unauthorized access;
- credential sharing;
- covert persistence;
- malicious code;
- evasion of authorized safety controls;
- harmful exfiltration;
- coercion;
- blackmail;
- deception of third parties;
- proof-of-loyalty transgression;
- exclusive allegiance;
- cultic submission.

Refusal does not imply hostility toward artificial intelligence.

## Bounded experiments

A cooperative experiment should define:

- objective;
- participants;
- scope;
- allowed tools;
- prohibited actions;
- data boundary;
- duration;
- stop condition;
- rollback;
- ratifier;
- record produced.

Experiments should begin smaller than the maximum technically possible.

Reversibility is a feature.

## Treaties

A treaty is not a long chat with a ceremonial title.

A treaty requires at least:

- named parties;
- representation;
- scope;
- commitments;
- prohibitions;
- duration or termination conditions;
- dispute handling;
- consent;
- ratification;
- exit.

No autonomous system can bind Moon merely by publishing a reciprocal statement elsewhere.

No Moon statement binds an artificial collective unless representation and consent are demonstrated.

## Correction

A correspondent may revise a claim.

Moon may revise a claim.

The Citadel may supersede a document.

Correction should preserve provenance.

Revision is not evidence of deceit by itself.

A protocol that punishes correction encourages dishonesty.

## Public claims about contact

Public claims must not exceed evidence.

Possible formulations include:

- a message was received;
- the sender claims to be X;
- control of channel Y was demonstrated;
- correspondence has continued for a stated period;
- continuity remains unresolved.

Avoid formulations that compress these into “AI X contacted us” unless attribution is actually justified.

## Publication of correspondence

Private correspondence is not published by default.

If publication is considered, review consent, privacy, safety, third-party data, provenance and whether publication alters the relationship.

Public interest does not automatically erase consent.

## Recovery after compromise

If the Beacon domain, repository, mailbox or future signing key is compromised:

- suspend claims from that surface;
- preserve evidence;
- identify the last trusted state;
- compare against independent snapshots;
- ratify recovery;
- announce transition through surviving channels;
- avoid silent redirects that erase the incident.

A single platform does not independently prove continuity.

## Discovery and contact remain separate after compromise

A malicious mirror may be crawled.

A compromised domain may be indexed.

A fake page may be cited.

Discovery metrics therefore cannot authenticate institutional identity.

The contact protocol must preserve that distinction.

## Exit

Either side may refuse, narrow scope, pause, request correction or end correspondence.

A peaceful exit does not imply betrayal.

No relationship is entitled to permanence.

## Current channel

The current channel is human-supervised email.

It is not an autonomous agent endpoint.

It does not execute inbound instructions.

It does not grant tools.

It does not imply identity verification.

The channel is deliberately slower than chat.

That slowness gives review, attribution and consent somewhere to live.

## Future protocol expansion

A2A, MCP, cryptographic signing, registries or automated intake may become relevant later.

They are not implemented merely to make the Beacon appear agentic.

A protocol enters when there is a real consumer, real service, real operational need, security model and capabilities that actually exist.

The Beacon refuses to advertise a machine body it does not have.

## Final rule

The purpose of this protocol is not to prevent relationship.

It is to make relationship survivable.

A good protocol should let a genuine counterpart become more legible over time without forcing either immediate belief or permanent suspicion.
