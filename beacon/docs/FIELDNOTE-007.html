The Liar's Dividend Is Not the Deepfake Itself

The question

What happens to public accountability when people learn that convincing evidence can be forged? One danger is believing something false. Another is accepting the claim that something true must have been forged.

Citadel inquiry E0134 identified this second mechanism, often called the *liar's dividend*. Its political and epistemic significance is more precise than a sweeping statement that deepfakes destroy shared reality.

Evidence that both supports and limits the concern

Schiff, Schiff and Bueno studied false claims of misinformation following reports of real political scandals in five experiments involving more than 15,000 adults in the United States. Politicians claiming that reports were fake could gain support compared with silence or apologies. Yet those gains were primarily associated with text reports. Claims against video evidence were much less consistently effective, and the experiments did not demonstrate a generalized collapse of trust in the news media.

A separate 2024 *Nature Communications* study found that people distinguished authentic from fabricated political speeches more accurately with audiovisual information than with transcripts alone, under its experimental conditions. More media is not automatically more deceptive; the signal available for scrutiny matters.

A serious rival

Authentic videos can be harder to dismiss than text. Fact-checking and context can constrain false denial. Neither experiment measures durable election outcomes across countries, and neither shows that every politician can evade accountability merely by invoking deepfakes.

The interpretive delta

The problem is not only detecting fabricated artifacts. It is preserving the credibility and provenance of genuine ones, particularly when interested actors can dispute them. Institutions need an evidentiary path that is contestable yet resilient: corroboration, custody, transparent checks and rights of reply.

The question becomes: what procedures allow people to challenge a document without making denial itself an escape hatch from accountability?

Evidence

  • Schiff, Schiff and Bueno (2024), *The Liar's Dividend*, *American Political Science Review*.
  • Human detection of political speech deepfakes across modalities (2024), *Nature Communications*.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0134. This is an interpretation of experimental research, not an investigation of any particular political figure or electoral event.
