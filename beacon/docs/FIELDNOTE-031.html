Moving Trees for Future Climate Is Not Just a Carbon Calculation

The question

Should reforestation always use local seed provenances, or can selecting trees adapted to anticipated climate conditions preserve forest functions under warming?

Citadel inquiry E0219 recognized that preserving today's species composition, maximizing future carbon storage and maintaining ecological relationships may require different decisions.

What the model suggests

Chakraborty and colleagues modeled assisted tree migration across seven major European species in a 2024 *Nature Climate Change* study. In the model, restricting planting to local seed provenances under future climate conditions could sharply reduce expected carbon uptake from regrowing forests. Selecting provenances adapted to projected future climates helped preserve or raise the modeled carbon sink.

That is an important scenario result, but it is not a large-scale randomized experiment showing successful establishment everywhere. Model outputs depend on climate projections, species distributions, seed characteristics and assumptions about survival and growth.

The serious competing concern

Local populations may carry ecological relationships and genetic characteristics not captured by a carbon metric. Moving material from elsewhere can introduce maladaptation, ecological disruption or new biosecurity risks. Assisted migration may also be costly and hard to reverse, particularly outside intensively managed plantations.

The interpretive delta

We replaced the binary *local or foreign seed* with a portfolio of conservation goals over time: survival, carbon, biodiversity, genetic diversity, ecological interaction and operational feasibility.

A good decision might combine local conservation, trials of future-adapted provenances, habitat protection and monitoring rather than imposing one planting rule on all forests.

Sources

  • Chakraborty et al. (2024), *Assisted tree migration can preserve the European forest carbon sink under climate change*, *Nature Climate Change*.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0219. European modeling is not a prediction for every Brazilian biome, and no original tree-migration field trial was conducted.
