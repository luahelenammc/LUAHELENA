A Right to Counsel Needs Lawyers, Not Just a Statute

The question

What happens when a city promises legal representation to tenants facing eviction but does not finance sufficient capacity to deliver it?

Citadel inquiry E0191 separated a legal entitlement on paper from its operational realization in crowded housing courts.

The observed institutional bottleneck

New York City adopted its Right to Counsel policy in 2017. In a May 2025 report, the city's Comptroller documented a sharp increase in active eviction cases and strained legal-services organizations. The report found that rates of tenant representation declined from 71% in the last quarter of fiscal 2021 to 42% in the last quarter of fiscal 2024, with especially severe declines in the Bronx.

The report also stated that 89% of represented households in fiscal 2024 avoided eviction. That is a descriptive outcome among represented households, not by itself an experimentally identified causal effect: the characteristics of represented and unrepresented cases can differ.

A necessary rival

Expanding a right can itself create pressure to fund services and improve court procedures over time. More lawyers are not the only intervention: diversion, mediation, housing assistance and prevention also matter. Caseload trends and court backlogs may reflect eviction policy, economic shocks and service-provider constraints together.

The interpretive delta

We moved from counting enacted rights to measuring effective access at the moment of need. A program's real coverage depends on lawyers, geographic distribution, preparation time, case complexity, continuity and whether represented tenants can meaningfully use their defense.

The institutional test is how a legal guarantee behaves when demand suddenly increases. Rights without capacity may remain normatively important while becoming unevenly available in practice.

Sources

  • NYC Comptroller (2025), *Evictions Up, Representation Down*.
  • Official Comptroller press summary and representation statistics.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0191. No individual tenants, case files or confidential litigation data are disclosed.
