A Rent Voucher Cannot Negotiate with Every Landlord

The question

If a family receives a rental voucher, why might it still fail to move to a neighborhood offering better opportunities?

Citadel inquiry E0215 located a missing institutional link between a subsidy and the ability to turn that subsidy into a successful tenancy. The answer is not always a stronger stated preference to stay in a lower-opportunity neighborhood.

What a randomized study found

Bergman, Chetty, DeLuca, Hendren, Katz and Palmer examined Seattle-area households receiving housing vouchers. Their randomized intervention combined information, financial support and customized housing-search assistance. In the evaluated population, the share of families moving to high-opportunity neighborhoods increased from approximately 15% to 53%. A second experiment pointed to customized search assistance as the principal driver of the effect.

That is a striking result within the program and population studied. It does not show that every municipality will achieve the same change, or that assistance by itself solves housing shortages, landlord discrimination, neighborhood preferences or rental-market affordability.

The meaningful objection

Information and individualized guidance can be expensive to deliver. Some households may reasonably prefer social ties, family proximity and familiar services. Landlords and markets differ, and a treatment effect for moves cannot be treated as proof of long-term outcomes for every child or adult.

The Citadel's interpretive delta

We separated formal eligibility from effective access. A legal entitlement or payment amount is not equivalent to finding an available unit, negotiating acceptance and securing a lease. Service capacity and power in the rental market determine how much of the promised choice is actually usable.

The better question is what combination of subsidy design, counseling, anti-discrimination enforcement and landlord participation changes real opportunities without erasing residents' own priorities.

Evidence

  • Bergman et al. (2024), *Creating Moves to Opportunity*, *American Economic Review*.
  • Seattle Housing Authority, Creating Moves to Opportunity program.

Provenance: Consolidated inquiry E0215. Interpretation of a context-specific randomized study, not a general estimate of residential mobility worldwide.
